# The object animation pattern

*Agreed with Matt on 2026-10-24, after the pyramid and the stalks. Every object is reworked this way.*

## 1. Say what the thing is

Write a short description of the object: what it is (a plant, a creature, a blob, an eye, a building, water),
what it is made of in the painting (its shapes and colours), and how it sits in the picture: what it grows out of,
leans on, overlaps, faces, and what is around it.

## 2. Decide what it gets to do

From what it is, choose how it can grow or change over time, in a way that changes the *drawing*, not only its
colour or size:

- plants grow leaves, shoots, tendrils, buds, flowers;
- creatures move: arms raise, mouths open, heads turn, tails curl, antennae extend;
- blobby things get blobbier: bulges grow, drips lengthen, lobes split off;
- eyes open and close, pupils look around;
- other things in the same spirit (water rises and splashes, clouds billow, buildings stack up).

The change maps onto the binding's timeline: the first frame is the painting exactly as drawn (the least of the
thing), the last frame is the most of it, and the frame shown follows the parameter's value or the note. Nothing
moves on its own.

## 3. Draw it the way the painting draws

Anything new is drawn in the painting's style:

- **Every form is an outlined shape with a fill. No bare lines.** A branch is a tapered green shape with a black
  outline, not a stroke; a new arm is an outlined, filled limb.
- **The ink matches the painting's** weight and character: the finer outlines (about 11 px) for small new
  features, heavier for big forms; round joins, slightly irregular, hand-drawn.
- **Colours come from the object itself or the painting's candy palette.**
- **New parts join seamlessly**: they start inside the shape they grow from and leave their outline open where
  they join, so there is no seam.
- **Redraw existing linework where the change needs it** (a mouth opening, an arm moving), not just add on top.

Supporting effects (sap rising, glow, colour moving toward a hue) can accompany the drawn change, but the drawn
change is the point.

## 4. Review loop, one object at a time (for now)

Until Matt says the choices can be made without him, every object goes through these steps with him:

1. **Look and describe.** Crop the object from the painting, say what it is and how it sits in the picture.
2. **Five end frames.** Mock up five different last frames (Drive or the note fully on) straight from the painting's
   layers, each a different idea of what the thing gets to do, all drawn the painting's way. Show them as one sheet
   next to the object as painted.
3. **Matt picks and adjusts.** He chooses one, combines several, or redirects.
4. **Progression.** Mock up the chosen end frame's frames 0 to 1: what happens first, what follows, what comes last.
   Show a strip of frames 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1 and a loop.
5. **Build** it into the app only after Matt approves the progression; render it from the app and compare it with the
   mockup.
6. **Log the choice** below: what was offered, what was picked, and what that says about his taste.

Once Matt hands it over, the loop runs without him, using the log to choose.

## 5. Matt's choices so far

What the decisions have shown, to apply to later objects:

- **The drawing must change, not just the colour.** Colour fills, glows and hue shifts alone were "not interesting
  enough"; the stalks only worked once they grew new drawn parts.
- **No bare lines; outlines around fills.** New parts are coloured shapes with black outlines, never single strokes.
- **Ink weight matches the object's own lines.** Heavy 26 px strokes on the thin-lined stalks were "too thick".
- **Growth in the object's own nature.** Plants sprout branches, leaves, tendrils and eye-buds. Lines merely getting
  bolder was not wanted.
- **Expressive, symbolic additions are welcome.** For the pyramid he chose a sunburst behind it and a big eye opening
  on its face, with a little flaming crown, over cracks, heat colour, puffing up, or an eruption.
- **Pyramid review (2026-10-24):** offered five end frames: flaming crown, puffed up, puffed and burning, erupting,
  sunburst and eye. Picked sunburst and eye "with a little bit of flaming crown". Before that, cracks of lava and a
  heat fill were rejected as not working; Matt stepped in to steer with options rather than iterations.
- **Creature review, rotor speed (2026-10-24):** the "spiral curl" is the ear of a sleepy green creature. Offered:
  propeller cap, pinwheel ear, dizzy, whirlwind, racing. Picked whirlwind, and asked that its little arm (the ribbed
  "baguette" under its cap) be raised as well. He knows his characters' anatomy: look for limbs and features that can
  move, not only for things to add around them.
- **Worm review, Expression (2026-10-24):** offered trumpet bell, rearing up, singing, blooming crest, ripples and
  bands. Picked three together: bell, crest, and ripples with bands. He likes layered, celebratory change (the
  painting is called a celebration): several of the object's features transforming at once is welcome when each
  arrives in its own part of the timeline.
- **Stream review, upper vibrato (2026-10-24):** the first five (waves, fountain, leaping fish, wavy water,
  whirlpool) "all suck". He pointed at the froth: make it bubbly and turn it into a little cloud. Lesson: find the
  object's most characterful small part and transform that, rather than effects spread over the whole object; and
  generic water motifs (waves, fish) read as clip-art. New shapes should carry the painting's pencil scribbles and
  lumpy, irregular outlines, not clean vector shapes. From round two he picked cloud and rainbow with bubble cloud,
  combined.
- **Eye review, percussion (2026-10-24, object picked by Claude):** offered wide awake, boing, ding sparkle, more
  eyes, starstruck. Picked boing: the playful, physical option (the eye springing out on a coil) over the merely
  decorative ones.
- **Cloud review, wheel age (2026-10-24, object picked by Claude):** offered old man cloud, billowing, stormy,
  overgrown, tonewheels. Picked overgrown (mushrooms and little flowers sprouting along its top, like moss on
  something left out a long time) straight away: age read as something living taking hold, not as decay or
  machinery.
- **Ghost review, lower drawbar 6 (2026-10-24, object picked by Claude):** offered Boo!, ram horns, party blower,
  waving, twins. Picked waving and twins together and said to build it without a separate progression review.
  Again the character doing something friendly and social (company arriving, a wave) over decoration. From here on,
  once a build is approved Claude picks the next object without asking.
- **Acorn frog review, round one (2026-10-24):** rejected all five (sprouting, acorn friends, croak, acorn pile, frog
  legs) for being too small: little fingers, toes, faces and leaves read as fiddly next to the painting's shapes.
  Two principles came out of it:
  - **New parts are the size of the painting's own shapes.** Added things should be roughly as big as the
    neighbouring forms (the acorns, the frog's head, the petals: about 150-300 px on the 4950 canvas), not small
    details on top of them.
  - **Small things can morph into something else.** A small object (the acorns) can turn into a different thing that
    could live there instead: a bulbous balloon cactus, graffiti-style eyeballs. Morphing replaces the shape; it
    doesn't decorate it.
- **Restraint with secondary ideas.** A "little bit" of crown: supporting elements stay smaller than the main one.

## 6. Repeat

For each object: render its current animation (`node scripts/object_frames.mjs <binding-id> --tag before`), run the
review loop above, build, render the after, compare first-to-last frame, and tune until it reads as the object doing
its thing in the painting's style.
