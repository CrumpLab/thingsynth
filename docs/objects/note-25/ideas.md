# note-25 C#1 (f141): ideas

**The shape:** a see-through yellow S-curve pour (#FFFB5B at 0.49, 297x431) falling from under the blue bell skewered
by the pink cross (whose pale lip is note-51), widening as it goes down and ending in a ragged, frilled hem on the grey
ground above the eye tree, with one inner ink line.

- **A, own-nature morph: honey.** The pour thickens to golden honey. 0.2 s: a fat drip bulges at its hem. 1-3 s: the
  yellow deepens to amber, drips lengthen one after another off the hem and fall as beads, and a lumpy honey puddle
  spreads below. 4 s: the longest drip lets go into the puddle with a splash.
- **B, character: a seahorse.** Its S-curve is already a seahorse's body. 0.2 s: its ragged foot starts to curl. 1-3
  s: the foot rolls up into a coiled tail, and a frilly candy fin ripples up its back, spines poking out along the
  front. 4 s: a snout and a waking eye show where it meets the bell.
- **C, playful pun: baritone sax.** Its S-curve is a saxophone's body, the blue bell is the mouthpiece, and the hem is
  its bell. 0.2 s: the hem flares round into a brass bell rim. 1-3 s: the body turns brass from the top down, and
  round candy keys pop on along it, one at a time. 4 s: a honking blast of fat lumpy crescents and candy notes bursts
  from the bell.

**Pick: C, baritone sax.** The pour's own S-curve changes into something else (rule 1), and it plays the note
literally as a big drawn thing (rule 4): a low honk for C#1. The keys arriving one by one give a beat every second, and
the blast at 4 s is the payoff. The sax is the shape itself turned brass, not a prop stuck on. A is the purest
own-nature idea but quieter. B overlaps the snail and nautilus spirals and needs the bell for its head.
