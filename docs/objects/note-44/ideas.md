# note-44 G#2 (f291): ideas

**What it is.** The left horn of the tall orange horned mask at the far top left (f291, blue-grey, 132x395): an S-curve
rising out of the mask's head and bending back up to the ochre tip (f293, note-83), close to the painting's left and
top edges, the open cream sky between the two horns.

- **A. Seahorse (own-nature morph).** 0.2 s: a snout pushes out of its top bend under the tip / 1-3 s: a frilly candy
  fin fans out along its back, its root curls into a coiled tail beside the mask's head, ridges band its belly / 4 s:
  a little coronet of spines and a round eye finish the seahorse standing on the mask's head.
- **B. Sock puppet (character).** 0.2 s: its upper end splits into a mouth / 1-3 s: two button eyes pop on, a tongue
  lolls, a tuft of yarn sprouts / 4 s: the mouth opens wide singing.
- **C. Horn of plenty (pun on horn + feast).** 0.2 s: its top end flares open into a dark horn mouth facing the gap
  between the horns / 1 s: a bunch of purple grapes tumbles out / 2 s: a lumpy pear / 3 s: an orange and a pink apple /
  4 s: a candy heap spilling into the gap, a sprig of flowers on top.

**Pick: C, horn of plenty.** The horn's own form does something (it flares open; rule 1) and it is a playful literal
pun drawn big (rule 4) that is also party food for the celebration (rule 3); a low-middle note gets a heavy pile that
builds one arrival a second. The seahorse is the runner-up (the S curve is already one), the sock puppet adds yet more
eyes and a mouth to the mask.
