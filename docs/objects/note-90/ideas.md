# note-90 F#6 (f114): ideas

**The shape:** a short magenta stalk segment, about 50 x 180 px, the trunk of the olive eye bush. It runs between the bottom of the bush's crown (the one with the half-lidded yellow-and-cyan eye) and the pale cushion mound it is planted in, beside a thin olive stalk.

- **A, catkins (own nature):** 0.2 s a fluffy pale-pink pompom bud pops out on the stalk's left side; 1-3 s more fuzzy catkins pop out in turn, alternating sides up the stalk, each about 90 px; 4 s the top one bursts into a ruff of fluff.
- **B, hummingbird (character):** 0.2 s a little magenta trumpet flower pops open on the stalk's side; 1-3 s a candy-green hummingbird zips in from the right, wings blurred in two lumpy crescents, its long beak dipping into the flower; 4 s it hovers, beak in, and a sparkle of nectar pops.
- **C, sharp (pun):** F# is a sharp, so it gets sharp. 0.2 s the first fat candy-pink thorn pops out of its side; 1-3 s thorns pop out one after another up both sides (six in all, about 70-100 px), and a green rosebud swells on a short side shoot; 4 s the bud pops open into a lumpy red rose about 150 px across.

**Pick: C, sharp.** It is a playful literal reading (rule 4) of the note's own name, drawn big. The stalk grows in its own nature (rule 1), and it ends on a celebratory bloom (rule 3). The thorns pop quick and bright, as a high note should. B brings a visitor and sits near the songbird's territory, and A is the quieter version of the same growth.
